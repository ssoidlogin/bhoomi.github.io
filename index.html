Why Does Bhoomi Show 'No Record Found'? 8 Real Reasons and How to Fix Them

By Masroor Ahmad | Land Records Guide
Updated 04 May 2026

You have opened the Bhoomi portal, carefully typed in your district, taluk, hobli, and survey number — and then it happens: "No Record Found." That three-word message can be deeply frustrating, especially if you urgently need your RTC (Record of Rights, Tenancy and Crops) document for a bank loan, court case, or property transaction.

The good news is that "No Record Found" does not always mean your land record is missing or deleted. In most cases, it means something small is wrong with the way the information was entered — or there is a data migration issue that can be resolved. In this article, we go through eight of the most common reasons why Bhoomi shows this error and, more importantly, what you can do about each one.

Reason 1: Incorrect or Partial Survey Number

Karnataka land survey numbers can be complex. Many survey numbers have sub-divisions written as fractions or with letters — for example, 45/2 or 67A. When users type these in the Bhoomi portal, a common mistake is:

  • Entering 45-2 instead of 45/2
  • Typing 67a (lowercase) instead of 67A
  • Including or excluding leading zeros (e.g., 007 vs 7)

Fix: Check your original sale deed, Pahani copy, or property tax document to get the exact survey number format. Try multiple variations if necessary.

Reason 2: Hobli Name Does Not Match

Karnataka is divided into districts, taluks, and hoblis. The Bhoomi database stores records under specific hobli names that may differ slightly from what you know or what appears on older documents. For example, a hobli might be recorded as "Yelahanka" in the database but your older document says "Yelahanaka" with a spelling variation.

Fix: When selecting your hobli from the dropdown menu on the Bhoomi portal, do not type it manually — select it from the list provided. If you are unsure which hobli your village belongs to, visit your local Taluk Office (Tehsildar's office) and ask the revenue officer.

Reason 3: Data Migration Errors from Old Records

The Bhoomi project digitized Karnataka's paper-based land records over many years. During this process, data entry errors occurred in thousands of records across the state. A survey number may have been entered incorrectly, a village name may have been misspelled, or an entire sub-division may have been accidentally missed during data entry.

This is one of the most common reasons for "No Record Found" on genuinely existing land — the land exists, but the data was not correctly migrated into the Bhoomi system.

Fix: File a formal complaint at your local Taluk Office using Form 9 (Correction of RTC). The revenue inspector will physically verify the land and correct the data. This process can take 30 to 90 days, but it is the only permanent solution for data migration errors.

Reason 4: Recent Land Division (Bifurcation) Not Yet Updated

When a piece of land is divided between family members or sold in parts, the division creates new survey numbers. If this bifurcation was approved by the revenue department but not yet updated in the Bhoomi digital database, the original survey number may show no record (if it was closed) while the new sub-numbers have also not yet appeared.

Fix: Check with your Taluk Office whether the mutation (Hakkku Badlavalike) and bifurcation order have been entered into the Bhoomi system. You can also check the MR (Mutation Register) copy from the portal or office to track where the record currently stands.

Reason 5: Land Under Court-Ordered Freeze or Encumbrance

In some cases, land records are temporarily locked or frozen in the Bhoomi system if there is a court-ordered stay, a government acquisition notice, or an ongoing boundary dispute. During this period, records may not appear in normal searches.

Fix: Consult a local advocate and check with the Taluk Office whether your land is under any legal hold. You can also apply for a certified copy of your RTC directly from the office even when the portal does not show it.

Reason 6: Wrong Selection of Old vs New Survey Numbers

After major land re-surveys, some areas in Karnataka received entirely new survey numbers under what is called "Re-survey" or "Resurvey" operations. If you are searching using the old survey number, it may no longer exist in the current database.

Fix: Ask at your Taluk Office for the re-survey concordance table — this is a list that maps old survey numbers to new ones. This document is not available online and must be obtained in person.

Reason 7: Village Name Recorded Differently

Karnataka has thousands of small villages, and many village names have been transliterated differently in the Bhoomi database compared to how locals write them. A village known locally as "Doddaballapura" might be stored as "Doddaballapur" — and this tiny difference is enough to produce a "No Record Found" result.

Fix: Try searching without your village name first — just use the district, taluk, and hobli, then browse through the village list that appears to find the exact spelling used in the Bhoomi database.

Reason 8: Server or Technical Glitch

Sometimes the problem is not your data — it is the portal itself. Bhoomi is a large government system serving millions of users and can experience outages, especially during peak hours, scheduled maintenance, or after software updates. During such times, the portal may return "No Record Found" for records that do exist and would otherwise display correctly.

Fix: Try accessing the portal at non-peak hours (early morning, after 9 PM, or on weekends). If the problem persists across multiple days, contact the Bhoomi helpdesk at 080-22113035 or email them through the official portal's feedback section.

When Should You Visit the Taluk Office in Person?

If you have tried all of the above fixes and still cannot find your record, visiting the Taluk Office in person is the most reliable next step. Bring the following documents:

  • Your original sale deed or gift deed
  • Previous RTC or Pahani copies (even photocopies help)
  • Property tax receipts (if available)
  • Aadhaar card for identity proof

The revenue inspector at the Taluk Office can access backend systems that are not available to the public and can locate your record even if the front-end portal is showing errors.

Remember: A "No Record Found" message is not the end of the road. In most cases, your land record exists — it just needs the right approach to find it.

i-RTC vs Regular RTC in Karnataka: Which One Do Banks, Courts, and Registrars Actually Accept?

If you have ever applied for a land-related loan or tried to submit an RTC document to a government office in Karnataka, you may have encountered a confusing situation: the official asked for an "i-RTC" specifically, and refused to accept the regular RTC printout you brought from home.

This happens more often than most citizens realize. Understanding the difference between these two versions of the same document — and knowing which situations require which — can save you significant time and frustration.

What is a Regular RTC (Pahani)?

A regular RTC (Record of Rights, Tenancy and Crops) is the standard land record document issued for agricultural land in Karnataka. It contains information about the land survey number, owner name, extent of land, type of land (wet, dry, garden), soil classification, irrigation source, crop details, revenue history, and any encumbrances or government orders affecting the land.

A regular RTC can be downloaded by anyone from the Bhoomi portal using just the survey number. There is no login required, no fees, and no authentication. This makes it easily accessible — but it also means it carries no official digital signature or verification stamp.

What is an i-RTC?

An i-RTC (Integrated RTC) is an enhanced, digitally authenticated version of the RTC. It was introduced by the Government of Karnataka to provide a tamper-proof, officially verifiable land record document. An i-RTC contains all the same information as a regular RTC, but additionally includes:

  • A unique QR code that can be scanned to verify authenticity online
  • A digital signature from the authorized revenue official
  • A unique verification number that links to the live government database
  • Date and time stamp of when the document was generated

An i-RTC is obtained only through the official Bhoomi portal after completing the required process, and in some cases requires a small fee. It is designed to replace the old practice of getting physically stamped RTC copies from the Taluk Office.

Which One Do Banks Accept for Agricultural Land Loans?

Most nationalized banks and cooperative banks in Karnataka — including State Bank of India, Canara Bank, Karnataka Vikas Grameena Bank, and others — now require the i-RTC specifically when processing agricultural land loans (Kisan Credit Cards, crop loans, land improvement loans).

This is because the i-RTC can be verified digitally by the bank's loan officer using the QR code, confirming that the document has not been altered and reflects the current status of the land. A regular RTC printout, being unverified, is not accepted for this purpose in most formal loan applications.

However, for preliminary applications or inquiry purposes (where you are just checking eligibility), a regular RTC is usually acceptable. Always confirm with your specific bank branch before visiting.

Which One Do Courts Accept?

For court proceedings involving land disputes, boundary conflicts, inheritance cases, or property transfers, courts in Karnataka generally accept both versions — but with a condition. A regular RTC submitted to a court must be accompanied by a certificate of authenticity from the Taluk Office. An i-RTC, because it contains a digital signature and QR verification, is generally accepted as self-authenticating.

In practice, many advocates and litigants now prefer to submit i-RTCs to courts because they reduce the chances of the opposing party challenging the document's authenticity.

What About the Sub-Registrar's Office?

For property registration transactions at the Sub-Registrar's office — such as sale deeds, gift deeds, or partition deeds — the i-RTC is strongly recommended. The Sub-Registrar's office verifies the RTC before registering a transaction to ensure the land is not under any dispute or encumbrance. The i-RTC's QR code enables faster and more reliable verification during this process.

Some Sub-Registrar offices have begun rejecting unverified regular RTC printouts and insisting on i-RTCs. This policy may vary by district and taluk, so check with your local office in advance.

Can Anyone Download an i-RTC?

Yes. Any citizen can download an i-RTC from the Bhoomi portal using the survey number and basic land details. The process involves selecting the "i-RTC" option on the portal, entering the required details, and making a nominal payment (usually between Rs. 10 and Rs. 25 depending on the number of pages). The document is then generated with the digital signature and QR code and can be downloaded as a PDF.

Unlike the regular RTC download, the i-RTC is valid for official purposes because of its embedded authentication. However, note that even the i-RTC has a validity concern — for critical transactions, many authorities prefer that the i-RTC be not more than three months old at the time of submission.

Quick Comparison Summary

Use a regular RTC for: General reference, understanding your land details, preliminary research, or informal purposes.

Use an i-RTC for: Bank loans, court proceedings, Sub-Registrar transactions, government scheme applications, and any formal official purpose where document verification is required.

When in doubt, always obtain and submit the i-RTC. It takes only a few minutes more and eliminates the risk of having your application rejected due to document format issues.

Land Mutation in Karnataka After Buying Property: A Complete Guide to Hakku Badlavalike

You have purchased a piece of land in Karnataka, registered the sale deed at the Sub-Registrar's office, and paid the stamp duty. Many people believe this completes the property purchase — but there is one more critical step that a large number of buyers skip, sometimes with serious long-term consequences: getting the land mutation done in the Bhoomi system.

Mutation, known in Kannada as "Hakku Badlavalike" (which literally translates to "change of rights"), is the process by which the name of the new owner is officially updated in the government's land revenue records. Without mutation, the Bhoomi portal will still show the previous owner's name — even though you legally purchased the land.

Why Is Mutation So Important?

Mutation is important for several reasons that directly affect you as a new landowner:

  • Your name will not appear on the RTC (Pahani) until mutation is completed, which means you cannot prove ownership through government records
  • Property tax assessment and receipts will continue to be issued in the previous owner's name
  • Banks may refuse to give you loans against the property if your name does not appear in revenue records
  • Future resale of the land can become legally complicated if mutation was never completed
  • Inheritance or transfer of the land to your legal heirs becomes harder without a clean mutation history

How Long After Purchase Should You Apply for Mutation?

Ideally, mutation should be applied for within 90 days of registering the sale deed. There is no formal legal deadline under Karnataka land law, but delaying mutation creates practical problems. If the seller passes away or becomes uncontactable, correcting errors later becomes significantly more difficult. Some Taluk Offices also note delayed mutations in the records, which can raise questions during future transactions.

Documents Required for Mutation Application

To apply for mutation (Hakku Badlavalike), you will need to submit the following documents at the Taluk Office or through the online Bhoomi portal (where available):

  • Certified copy of the registered Sale Deed (obtained from the Sub-Registrar's office)
  • Latest RTC (Pahani) of the land showing the previous owner's name
  • Property tax receipt in the name of the previous owner
  • Sketch or map of the land (if required by the Taluk Office)
  • Aadhaar card of the new owner (buyer) for identity proof
  • Affidavit (in some cases, if required by the revenue authority)

What Happens After You Apply?

Once you submit your mutation application at the Taluk Office, the following process takes place:

1. Site Verification

The Revenue Inspector (RI) will visit the site to physically verify the land and confirm the details in the sale deed against the actual survey marks and boundaries. This site visit usually happens within 30 to 45 days of application.

2. Public Notice Period

A public notice is issued for 30 days, inviting any objections from other interested parties (such as other claimants to the land). If no objections are received, the mutation is approved.

3. Passing the Mutation Order

The Tahsildar (Taluk Magistrate) reviews the application and, if satisfied, passes a Mutation Order (MO). This order officially transfers the land record to the new owner's name.

4. Database Update

The Bhoomi database is then updated, and the new RTC reflecting your name as owner is generated. The MR (Mutation Register) copy, which documents the full mutation history of the land, is also updated.

What If the Mutation Is Rejected or Objected To?

If someone files an objection during the 30-day public notice period — such as another claimant, a family member of the seller, or a co-owner — the mutation process enters a dispute resolution phase. The Tahsildar will hold a hearing where both parties present their case. Based on the evidence, the Tahsildar will make a decision.

If you believe a rejection or objection is unjust, you have the right to appeal to the Assistant Commissioner of the revenue division. A lawyer specializing in property law can be very helpful in such situations.

Online Mutation Applications

Karnataka has made progress in enabling online mutation applications through the Bhoomi portal for certain categories of transactions. However, as of now, most complex cases — including agricultural land sales, inherited land, and disputed properties — still require in-person visits to the Taluk Office. Check the Bhoomi portal for the latest information on which mutation types can be processed online in your district.

Whether you apply online or in person, always keep physical copies of every document you submit and every receipt you receive. The mutation process can sometimes take 3 to 6 months, and having all paperwork organized will help you follow up effectively.

Encumbrance Certificate (EC) in Karnataka: What It Is, Why You Need It, and How to Get It

Before you buy any property in Karnataka — whether it is agricultural land, a residential plot, or a commercial building — one document is absolutely non-negotiable: the Encumbrance Certificate, commonly called the EC.

Yet a surprising number of property buyers in Karnataka either do not know what an EC is, do not know how to read one, or skip it entirely — putting themselves at serious financial and legal risk. This article explains everything you need to know about the EC in Karnataka, written in plain language without legal jargon.

What is an Encumbrance Certificate?

An Encumbrance Certificate is an official government document that shows whether a piece of property has any legal or financial liabilities attached to it. In simple terms, it tells you:

  • Whether the property has been mortgaged or used as security for a loan
  • Whether there are any registered disputes, court orders, or attachments on the property
  • Whether there have been any recent sale transactions involving the property
  • A list of all registered transactions involving the property for the period you request

The EC is issued by the Sub-Registrar's office and covers a specified period — for example, the last 13 years or the last 30 years. You can choose the period based on how thorough your due diligence needs to be.

Why Is the EC So Critical Before Buying Property?

Imagine buying a piece of agricultural land for Rs. 50 lakhs, only to discover a year later that the seller had taken a loan of Rs. 20 lakhs against that very land before selling it to you — and the bank now has a lien on your property. This situation, unfortunately, happens more often than most people realize.

A clean EC (meaning no encumbrances listed for the requested period) gives you confidence that the property has no financial liabilities. Banks also insist on a clean EC before sanctioning any property loan — they need to verify that the property being offered as collateral is free of prior claims.

Two Types of EC in Karnataka

In Karnataka, there are two forms of EC that the Sub-Registrar's office can issue:

  • Form 15 (EC with Encumbrances): This is issued when the office records show that the property has had registered transactions during the requested period — such as sales, mortgages, or gifts. Each transaction is listed with details.
  • Form 16 (Nil EC): This is issued when the property records show absolutely no registered transactions during the requested period. A Form 16 EC is also called a "Nil Encumbrance Certificate" and is considered the strongest possible form of clean title for that period.

How to Apply for EC in Karnataka (Online via Kaveri Portal)

Karnataka has moved the EC application process online through the Kaveri Online Services portal. Here is how the process works:

  • Visit the Kaveri Online Services website and register as a new user (or log in if you already have an account).
  • Select the Encumbrance Certificate option and fill in the property details including district, taluk, village, survey number, and the period for which you want the EC.
  • Pay the prescribed fee online (fees vary based on the period requested — typically Rs. 25 to Rs. 200 for standard periods).
  • The EC is usually generated within 1 to 3 working days and can be downloaded from the portal.

Note: The online EC from the Kaveri portal is digitally signed and considered valid for most official purposes. However, some older banks and courts still require a physically stamped EC from the Sub-Registrar's office. Always confirm acceptance with the institution before you apply.

How Far Back Should Your EC Go?

For routine property purchases, most advocates recommend requesting an EC covering the last 13 years — this covers the typical loan repayment periods of most mortgages. For larger or higher-risk transactions (such as agricultural land in contested areas or properties involved in past disputes), requesting 30 years of EC history provides greater protection.

If you are getting a home loan, the bank will usually specify how many years of EC they require. Follow their requirement exactly.

What If the EC Shows an Encumbrance?

If the EC shows a mortgage or loan, ask the seller to provide a loan closure certificate from the bank confirming that the loan was repaid. If the EC shows an old sale transaction that was not followed by mutation, investigate further with a qualified advocate before proceeding with your purchase.

Never ignore what the EC says. Every entry listed has a reason, and understanding each entry is critical to protecting your investment.

What You Need to Know Before Buying or Renting

RTC vs. Mutation Register: What's the Difference?

One of the most common points of confusion for new property buyers in Karnataka is the difference between an RTC (Pahani) and a Mutation Register (MR) extract. While both are available on the Bhoomi portal, they serve very different legal purposes.

The RTC (Record of Rights, Tenancy, and Crops) is a snapshot of the current status of the land. It tells you who owns the land right now, what crops are being grown, the total area, and if there are any active bank loans (liabilities) tied to the property.

The Mutation Register Extract, on the other hand, is the historical log of how that land changed hands. Whenever a property is sold, inherited, or divided, a "Mutation" occurs. The MR extract proves the legal chain of title. If you are buying land, banks and lawyers will demand the MR extracts for the last 15 to 30 years to ensure the current owner listed on the RTC actually obtained the land legally.

Karnataka Land Measurement System

When looking at a Bhoomi RTC, you will rarely see measurements in square feet. Karnataka's revenue department uses a traditional system heavily reliant on the "Gunta." Understanding how to convert these figures is essential to ensuring you get exactly what you pay for.

The Baseline: In Karnataka, 1 Gunta is exactly 1,089 square feet. This is the fundamental unit for agricultural land sub-divisions.

  • 40 Guntas = 1 Acre (which equals 43,560 square feet).
  • 2.47 Acres = 1 Hectare.

The "Anna" System: In older, handwritten property documents (prior to the digital Bhoomi system), you might also see fractions written in "Annas." In this context, 16 Annas makes up one whole share of the land. If an ancestral property says a sibling owns an "8 Anna share," it means they own exactly 50% of that survey number.

How Stamp Duty is Calculated in Karnataka

Property registration in Karnataka involves three distinct charges: the Basic Stamp Duty, a Surcharge/Cess, and the Registration Fee. These are calculated on either the actual sale price or the government's "Guidance Value"—whichever is higher.

1. Basic Stamp Duty: For properties valued over ₹45 Lakhs, the standard basic stamp duty is currently around 5.6%. For affordable housing (under ₹45 Lakhs), the government occasionally offers reduced brackets (like 2% or 3%), so it is vital to check the latest state budget updates.

2. Surcharges (BMRDA/BBMP/Village): On top of the basic duty, a surcharge is applied depending on where the land is located. This is typically 10% of the basic stamp duty itself, not the property value. It funds local infrastructure like rural development or city transport.

3. Registration Fee: Finally, a flat 1% of the total property value is charged by the Sub-Registrar's office for the administrative process of updating the Kaveri portal and initiating the mutation process into the Bhoomi system.

Why is the RTC (Pahani) Document Important?

The RTC contains vital details about a specific piece of land:

  • Owner Details: Who currently owns the land.
  • Tenancy: Who is cultivating the land.
  • Crops: What crops are grown (essential for crop insurance).
  • Land Type: Agricultural, commercial, or non-agricultural.
  • Liabilities: Existing bank loans or litigations on the land.

How to Check Bhoomi RTC (Pahani) Online

Follow this step-by-step guide to view your land records on the Karnataka Bhoomi portal.

Step 1: Visit the Portal
Go to the official Bhoomi Land Records website (landrecords.karnataka.gov.in).

Step 2: Select 'View RTC and MR'
On the homepage, look for the "Services" section and click on "View RTC and MR".

Step 3: Enter Land Details
You will be asked to fill in the following details based on where your land is located: District, Taluk, Hobli, Village.

Step 4: Survey Number
Enter the Survey Number of your land. Once entered, the system will ask for the Surnoc and Hissa Number (sub-division number).

Step 5: Fetch Details
Click on "Fetch Details". The complete land record (RTC) will be displayed on your screen. You can view owner names, land extent, and soil type here.

Note: To download a signed legal copy for court or loan purposes, you must use the "i-RTC" wallet service and pay a nominal fee (approx. ₹15).

What is a Digitally Signed i-RTC?

Unlike the informational copy, a Digitally Signed i-RTC is a legal document valid for court proceedings, bank loan applications, and property registration. It carries a unique QR code and digital signature that verifies its authenticity.

Steps to Download Signed i-RTC:

  1. Login: Visit the Bhoomi portal and select "i-RTC Wallet Services". You must log in with your User ID (or create a new account).
  2. Add Money: The signed copy is a paid service (approx. ₹15 per copy). You must top up your wallet using UPI, Net Banking, or Credit Card.
  3. Search Land: Enter your District, Taluk, Hobli, Village, and Survey Number.
  4. Pay & Print: Click "Pay and Print." The PDF will download immediately.

Save this PDF securely; the download link may expire.

The Bhoomi server often undergoes maintenance between 10 PM and 12 AM. If you are trying to generate an i-RTC (certified copy), we recommend doing it during morning hours for a faster payment gateway response.

How to Check Mutation Status (MR) Online

If you have recently bought or sold land, you need to check if the ownership transfer (Mutation) has been updated in the government records.

  • Navigate to the "View RTC and MR" section.
  • Select the "Mutation Status" tab.
  • Enter the District, Taluk, Hobli, Village, and Survey Number.
  • Click "Fetch Details".

The system will show the current status of your mutation application (e.g., Pending, Accepted, or Rejected).

How to View Village Revenue Maps Online?

A Revenue Map shows the geographical boundaries of a village and the division of survey numbers within it. It helps landowners identify the shape and location of their plot within the village layout.

How to Download:

  1. Go to the "Revenue Maps" section on the Bhoomi home page.
  2. Select your District, Taluk, and Hobli.
  3. Choose your Village from the list.
  4. Click the PDF icon to download the full village map.

These maps are large files; ensure you have a stable internet connection.

What is the Dishaank App?

Developed by the Karnataka Remote Sensing Applications Centre (KSRSAC), the Dishaank App allows you to see your survey number based on your current GPS location. This is incredibly useful for verifying if a plot of land you are buying actually falls under the survey number claimed by the seller.

How to Use:

  • Download Dishaank from the Google Play Store.
  • Enable GPS/Location services on your phone.
  • Stand on the land in question and open the app.
  • The map will overlay the survey boundaries on your location, showing you the exact Survey Number and Gomala (government land) details.

How to Verify XML RTC

Why Verify? Fraudsters can alter printed documents. The "Verify XML" feature ensures that the document you hold matches the data on the government server exactly.

Steps:

  1. Go to the "RTC XML Verification" tab on the portal.
  2. Enter the Reference Number found on your downloaded i-RTC.
  3. The system will confirm if the document is genuine and display the valid data for comparison.

Technical & Access Issues

The portal handles massive traffic, which can lead to slow loading times or "Server Not Found" errors.

  • Browser Compatibility: The website works best on Google Chrome or Mozilla Firefox. Avoid using older versions of Internet Explorer.
  • Clear Cache: If the page isn't loading correctly or buttons aren't clicking, clear your browser’s Cache and Cookies (Ctrl+Shift+Delete) and restart the browser.
  • Pop-up Blockers: The RTC is usually generated in a new window. If nothing happens when you click "View" or "Download," check your browser’s address bar for a blocked pop-up icon and select "Always allow."
  • OTP Not Received: During registration or login, if you don't receive an OTP, wait 2 minutes before clicking "Resend." Ensure your mobile number is not on a DND (Do Not Disturb) list.

Sometimes the website works fine, but the information displayed is incorrect or missing.

  • Name or Measurement Mismatch: If the owner's name is spelled wrong or the land area is incorrect, this cannot be fixed online. You must visit the Tahsildar office (Taluk level) with your original Sale Deed to apply for a Rectification Mutation.
  • Mutation Status "Pending": If you recently bought land, it takes roughly 45–60 days for the mutation process to reflect on the RTC. You can track this under the "Mutation Status" tab using your transaction ID.

Land Record Management in Karnataka

While the previous sections covered the operational mechanics of the Bhoomi portal—how to click, view, and download—effective land management in Karnataka requires a deeper understanding of the administrative ecosystem. The digitization of records is a monumental step, yet the interface between digital data and ground reality is where most citizens face challenges. The following guide addresses advanced scenarios, strategic troubleshooting, and the "unwritten" protocols of the Revenue Department to help you safeguard your assets effectively.

1. Understanding the Mutation Lifecycle

When you purchase land or inherit property, the change of name (Mutation) is not instantaneous, even if the system is digital. Understanding the human hierarchy behind the "Pending" status can help you intervene effectively when delays occur.

The Journey of a File

Once a transaction is registered at the Sub-Registrar's office (Kaveri portal), the data is pushed to Bhoomi. However, the approval process moves through specific tiers of the Revenue Department:

  • The Village Accountant (VA): The first point of contact. The VA verifies the transaction against existing records. If your application is stuck, it is often pending the VA's initial "Checklist" approval.
  • The Revenue Inspector (RI): The critical field officer. The RI is mandated to visit the property, verify possession, and serve notices to interested parties. Strategic Insight: If your status says "Pending for RI Inspection" for more than 30 days, a physical visit to the Nadakacheri to schedule the inspection is often necessary.
  • The Shirastedar: A supervisory check to ensure no legal disputes (court stays) are active on the survey number.
  • The Tahsildar: The final approving authority who digitally signs the mutation order.

Common Failure Point: The "J-Slip" mismatch. Sometimes, the data sent from the Sub-Registrar (Kaveri) does not perfectly match the Bhoomi database (e.g., a spelling error in the seller's name). This causes the auto-mutation to fail, requiring a manual "Rectification Application" at the Bhoomi center.

2. RTC: Advanced Column Analysis

Most users look only at Column 9 (Owner Name) and Column 12 (Crops). However, the legal health of your land is hidden in the obscure columns of the Pahani (RTC). Ignoring these can lead to disastrous valuation issues during resale.

Column 3: Nature of Possession & Tenancy

This is the most litigious column. If you see names listed here other than the owner, or references to "Genide" (Tenancy), it implies protected tenant rights under the Land Reforms Act. Warning: Even if you buy land with a clear Title Deed, if Column 3 has a legacy tenant entry that was not formally cleared by the Land Tribunal, you may be unable to convert the land or sell it. Always ensure Column 3 is clear or explicitly states "Self" (Swantha).

Column 9: The "Pyki" Complication

In ancestral properties, you may see the term "Pyki" next to an extent of land. This indicates a share within a larger undivided survey number. The Risk: "Pyki" entries are often vague regarding specific boundaries. You might own 1 acre "Pyki" in a 10-acre survey number, but which 1 acre? Without a "Tatkal Podi" (sub-division sketch) to fix the boundaries, "Pyki" owners are vulnerable to boundary encroachments by neighbors.

Column 11: Liabilities and Court Stays

Banks mark their liens here (hypothecation). However, look for "Court Stay" or "Injunction" notes. Sometimes, a stay order from a decades-old civil suit remains on the record long after the case is resolved because no one applied to remove it. You must file a request with a certified copy of the court judgment to the Tahsildar to clean this column.

3. "Phut Kharab": Why Your Map and RTC Don't Match

A frequent source of anxiety for landowners is discovering that their physical land measurement does not match the RTC extent. This is often due to "Phut Kharab" (B-Kharab) land.

Class A Kharab: Land that is unfit for agriculture due to geographical reasons (rocky terrain, water logging). This land belongs to you and can be converted for non-agricultural use.

Class B Kharab: Land reserved for public use (footpaths, cart tracks, canals, burial grounds). This land belongs to the government, even if it falls inside your survey boundaries. Strategic Implication: If your RTC says you own 2 Acres, but 0.10 Guntas is classified as "B-Kharab," you effectively only own 1.30 Acres for development purposes. You cannot build a wall around B-Kharab land. When buying land, always subtract B-Kharab from the total extent to calculate the true usable value.

4. Navigating Land Conversion: The Shift from Bhoomi to E-Swathu

Bhoomi manages agricultural land. Once you decide to build a house or a factory, you must convert the land use (DC Conversion). This transition is complex and often misunderstood.

The Transition Phase:

  1. Application: You apply for conversion online via the Revenue Department.
  2. Reports: The file is routed to the Urban Development Authority (CDP plan verification), Pollution Control Board, and Fire Department.
  3. The "Order": Once the Deputy Commissioner (DC) issues the Conversion Order, the land is technically no longer "Agricultural."

The Crucial Step: Once converted, the land record must eventually move out of Bhoomi and into E-Swathu (for rural panchayats) or E-Aasthi/UPOR (for urban municipal corporations). The Gap: Many owners get the conversion order but fail to mutate the record in the local municipality. This leaves the land in a "limbo" state—it appears as agricultural in Bhoomi (causing tax issues) but is legally residential. You must actively apply to the Panchayat/Municipality to create a "Form 9/11" (Khata) to finalize this shift.

5. Succession and Inheritance: The "Vamshavruksha" Protocol

Inheritance mutation (Pavati) is distinct from sale mutation. It relies heavily on the "Vamshavruksha" (Family Tree) certificate. A common bottleneck occurs when family members are estranged or abroad.

The "No Objection" Requirement:

If a father passes away leaving land to three children, the RTC will typically list all three names jointly. If one sibling wants to sell their share, or if the family wants to divide the property (Partition/Vibhag), all members must be present to sign or provide a registered Power of Attorney (POA). Pro Tip: If a legal heir is missing or uncooperative, the Revenue Department cannot adjudicate. You must approach the Civil Court for a "Partition Suit." Revenue officers are strictly administrative; they cannot decide on disputed inheritance claims.

6. Fixing Legacy Errors: The Akarband and Tippan

Sometimes the RTC is right, but the map is wrong. Or the map is right, but the RTC is wrong. In Karnataka, the "Mother Document" for measurements is not the RTC, but the Akarband.

Akarband: The final register of area and assessment. It was established during the original survey (often decades ago). Tippan: The field book containing the original hand-drawn sketches and measurements of the survey number.

Strategy: If you face a boundary dispute where a neighbor claims you have encroached on their land, or if the digital map in Dishaank seems shifted, do not rely solely on the RTC. Apply for a certified copy of the Tippan and Akarband from the Survey Department. These documents hold higher evidentiary value in court regarding boundaries than the Bhoomi RTC.

7. Security: Protecting Your Land from Digital Fraud

Digitization has reduced manual tampering, but "Identity Theft" in land records is a new threat. Fraudsters may present fake IDs to initiate a mutation or change the mobile number linked to the account.

The "Watch Mode" Strategy

Land is a passive asset, often ignored for years. This negligence is dangerous. Adopt a "Digital Watch" routine:

  • Quarterly Checks: Log into Bhoomi every three months to view your RTC. Check the "Mutation Status" tab even if you haven't applied for anything. If you see a "Pending" application you didn't initiate, file an objection with the Tahsildar immediately.
  • SMS Alerts: Ensure your current mobile number is linked to your Aadhaar and the land record. The system generates SMS alerts for new mutation requests. If you change your phone number, update it in the Bhoomi database via the outcome of a small correction application.

8. The "Podi" Service: Moving from Joint to Individual Ownership

A vast majority of rural litigation stems from "Joint Ownership" in a single Survey Number. If you and your brother own 2 acres each in Survey No. 10 (Total 4 acres), the RTC shows both names. This is legally weak.

The Solution: Tatkal Podi

You must apply for "Podi" (Sub-division). Government surveyors will come, measure the specific 2 acres you possess, and assign a new subdivision number (e.g., Survey No. 10/1 and 10/2). Why do it? Banks are increasingly refusing loans on "Joint" RTCs. Furthermore, without Podi, you cannot sell a specific piece of the land clearly. The "Tatkal Podi" scheme is a fast-track method (paid service) to get this done, usually within weeks compared to the months taken by the regular process.

9. Dispute Resolution Hierarchy

If the Tahsildar passes an order you disagree with (e.g., rejecting your mutation), do not rush to the High Court. The Revenue Act provides a specific appellate hierarchy:

Authority Role Time Limit for Appeal
Assistant Commissioner (AC) First Appellate Authority. Handles disputes regarding mutation orders and title entries. Usually within 60 days of the Tahsildar's order.
Deputy Commissioner (DC) Second Appellate Authority. Reviews the AC's decisions and handles major conversion disputes. Within 60 days of the AC's order.
Karnataka Appellate Tribunal (KAT) The final revenue court before the High Court. Varies, usually 90 days.

Note: Skipping these steps and going directly to the Civil Court is often rejected on the grounds of "Exhaustion of Administrative Remedies." However, for questions of title (who is the true owner), the Civil Court is the only authority; Revenue Courts only decide whose name goes in the record based on summary evidence.

10. Missing Records: When the System Says "Data Not Found"

A terrifying scenario for an owner is entering their survey number and seeing "Data Not Found." This usually happens for two reasons:

  1. The "UDR" (Update Data Registry) Issue: During the massive digitization drive in the early 2000s, some records were missed or entered with typos. These are "Missing RTCs." You must file an application at the Bhoomi center with your old manual (handwritten) Pahani to re-digitize the record.
  2. Survey Number Changes: Sometimes, during a re-survey, survey numbers are re-mapped or re-numbered. Use the "Village Map" to visually locate your land and identify the new survey number assigned to that geographic location.

11. Practical Tips for NRI/Out-of-State Owners

Managing Karnataka land from outside the state is difficult. The "Power of Attorney" (POA) is your most vital tool, but it has limitations.

  • Adjudication: A POA executed abroad (e.g., in the USA) is not immediately valid in India. It must be "Adjudicated" (stamped) at the District Registrar's office in Karnataka to be legally acceptable for property transactions or court appearances.
  • Life Certificate: If a senior citizen is the owner and cannot visit the office, some revenue services allow a video-KYC or a Life Certificate attested by a Gazetted Officer to process requests, though this depends heavily on the discretion of the local Tahsildar.

Summary Checklist for Healthy Land Records

  • Does your RTC match your physical possession?
  • Is the "Owner Name" spelled exactly as it appears in your Aadhaar/PAN? (Crucial for future sales).
  • Is the "Khata" (Account) number unique to you, or shared with others?
  • Have you downloaded the "Mutations" history to ensure the chain of title is unbroken from the previous owner to you?
  • Is the "Soil Type" and "Land Use" accurate? (Incorrect classification can lead to penalties).

By treating your digital land record as a living document that requires regular "health checks," you can avoid the nightmarish delays that plague property transactions. The Bhoomi system is powerful, but it rewards the vigilant.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Q: Can I use the online RTC copy for legal purposes?
A: The free viewable version is for information only. For legal matters, bank loans, or registration, you must download the Digitally Signed i-RTC, which is valid without a manual signature.

Q: What if my land details are missing on Bhoomi?
A: If your data is not digitized or contains errors, you must visit the nearest Bhoomi Monitoring Cell or your Taluk office to file an application for data entry or correction.

Q: How can I view the Revenue Map of my village?
A: Visit the "Revenue Maps" section on the Bhoomi portal, select your district/taluk/village, and you can download the PDF map showing survey number boundaries.
