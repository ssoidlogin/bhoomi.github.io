Bhoomi

Bhoomi is the official digital land records management system launched by the Government of Karnataka. It was created to digitize paper records, making them transparent, tamper-proof, and easily accessible to citizens.

The most important document you can access here is the RTC (Record of Rights, Tenancy and Crops), locally known as Pahani. Whether you are a farmer applying for a loan or a landowner verifying property details, the Bhoomi portal eliminates the need to visit government offices, allowing you to access records from your home.

What You Need to Know Before Buying or Renting

RTC vs. Mutation Register: What's the Difference?

One of the most common points of confusion for new property buyers in Karnataka is the difference between an RTC (Pahani) and a Mutation Register (MR) extract. While both are available on the Bhoomi portal, they serve very different legal purposes.

The RTC (Record of Rights, Tenancy, and Crops) is a snapshot of the current status of the land. It tells you who owns the land right now, what crops are being grown, the total area, and if there are any active bank loans (liabilities) tied to the property.

The Mutation Register Extract, on the other hand, is the historical log of how that land changed hands. Whenever a property is sold, inherited, or divided, a "Mutation" occurs. The MR extract proves the legal chain of title. If you are buying land, banks and lawyers will demand the MR extracts for the last 15 to 30 years to ensure the current owner listed on the RTC actually obtained the land legally.

Karnataka Land Measurement System

When looking at a Bhoomi RTC, you will rarely see measurements in square feet. Karnataka's revenue department uses a traditional system heavily reliant on the "Gunta." Understanding how to convert these figures is essential to ensuring you get exactly what you pay for.

The Baseline: In Karnataka, 1 Gunta is exactly 1,089 square feet. This is the fundamental unit for agricultural land sub-divisions.

  • 40 Guntas = 1 Acre (which equals 43,560 square feet).
  • 2.47 Acres = 1 Hectare.

The "Anna" System: In older, handwritten property documents (prior to the digital Bhoomi system), you might also see fractions written in "Annas." In this context, 16 Annas makes up one whole share of the land. If an ancestral property says a sibling owns an "8 Anna share," it means they own exactly 50% of that survey number.

How Stamp Duty is Calculated in Karnataka

Property registration in Karnataka involves three distinct charges: the Basic Stamp Duty, a Surcharge/Cess, and the Registration Fee. These are calculated on either the actual sale price or the government's "Guidance Value"—whichever is higher.

1. Basic Stamp Duty: For properties valued over ₹45 Lakhs, the standard basic stamp duty is currently around 5.6%. For affordable housing (under ₹45 Lakhs), the government occasionally offers reduced brackets (like 2% or 3%), so it is vital to check the latest state budget updates.

2. Surcharges (BMRDA/BBMP/Village): On top of the basic duty, a surcharge is applied depending on where the land is located. This is typically 10% of the basic stamp duty itself, not the property value. It funds local infrastructure like rural development or city transport.

3. Registration Fee: Finally, a flat 1% of the total property value is charged by the Sub-Registrar's office for the administrative process of updating the Kaveri portal and initiating the mutation process into the Bhoomi system.

Why is the RTC (Pahani) Document Important?

The RTC contains vital details about a specific piece of land:

  • Owner Details: Who currently owns the land.
  • Tenancy: Who is cultivating the land.
  • Crops: What crops are grown (essential for crop insurance).
  • Land Type: Agricultural, commercial, or non-agricultural.
  • Liabilities: Existing bank loans or litigations on the land.

How to Check Bhoomi RTC (Pahani) Online

Follow this step-by-step guide to view your land records on the Karnataka Bhoomi portal.

Step 1: Visit the Portal
Go to the official Bhoomi Land Records website (landrecords.karnataka.gov.in).

Step 2: Select 'View RTC and MR'
On the homepage, look for the "Services" section and click on "View RTC and MR".

Step 3: Enter Land Details
You will be asked to fill in the following details based on where your land is located: District, Taluk, Hobli, Village.

Step 4: Survey Number
Enter the Survey Number of your land. Once entered, the system will ask for the Surnoc and Hissa Number (sub-division number).

Step 5: Fetch Details
Click on "Fetch Details". The complete land record (RTC) will be displayed on your screen. You can view owner names, land extent, and soil type here.

Note: To download a signed legal copy for court or loan purposes, you must use the "i-RTC" wallet service and pay a nominal fee (approx. ₹15).

What is a Digitally Signed i-RTC?

Unlike the informational copy, a Digitally Signed i-RTC is a legal document valid for court proceedings, bank loan applications, and property registration. It carries a unique QR code and digital signature that verifies its authenticity.

Steps to Download Signed i-RTC:

  1. Login: Visit the Bhoomi portal and select "i-RTC Wallet Services". You must log in with your User ID (or create a new account).
  2. Add Money: The signed copy is a paid service (approx. ₹15 per copy). You must top up your wallet using UPI, Net Banking, or Credit Card.
  3. Search Land: Enter your District, Taluk, Hobli, Village, and Survey Number.
  4. Pay & Print: Click "Pay and Print." The PDF will download immediately.

Save this PDF securely; the download link may expire.

The Bhoomi server often undergoes maintenance between 10 PM and 12 AM. If you are trying to generate an i-RTC (certified copy), we recommend doing it during morning hours for a faster payment gateway response.

How to Check Mutation Status (MR) Online

If you have recently bought or sold land, you need to check if the ownership transfer (Mutation) has been updated in the government records.

  • Navigate to the "View RTC and MR" section.
  • Select the "Mutation Status" tab.
  • Enter the District, Taluk, Hobli, Village, and Survey Number.
  • Click "Fetch Details".

The system will show the current status of your mutation application (e.g., Pending, Accepted, or Rejected).

How to View Village Revenue Maps Online?

A Revenue Map shows the geographical boundaries of a village and the division of survey numbers within it. It helps landowners identify the shape and location of their plot within the village layout.

How to Download:

  1. Go to the "Revenue Maps" section on the Bhoomi home page.
  2. Select your District, Taluk, and Hobli.
  3. Choose your Village from the list.
  4. Click the PDF icon to download the full village map.

These maps are large files; ensure you have a stable internet connection.

What is the Dishaank App?

Developed by the Karnataka Remote Sensing Applications Centre (KSRSAC), the Dishaank App allows you to see your survey number based on your current GPS location. This is incredibly useful for verifying if a plot of land you are buying actually falls under the survey number claimed by the seller.

How to Use:

  • Download Dishaank from the Google Play Store.
  • Enable GPS/Location services on your phone.
  • Stand on the land in question and open the app.
  • The map will overlay the survey boundaries on your location, showing you the exact Survey Number and Gomala (government land) details.

How to Verify XML RTC

Why Verify? Fraudsters can alter printed documents. The "Verify XML" feature ensures that the document you hold matches the data on the government server exactly.

Steps:

  1. Go to the "RTC XML Verification" tab on the portal.
  2. Enter the Reference Number found on your downloaded i-RTC.
  3. The system will confirm if the document is genuine and display the valid data for comparison.

Technical & Access Issues

The portal handles massive traffic, which can lead to slow loading times or "Server Not Found" errors.

  • Browser Compatibility: The website works best on Google Chrome or Mozilla Firefox. Avoid using older versions of Internet Explorer.
  • Clear Cache: If the page isn't loading correctly or buttons aren't clicking, clear your browser’s Cache and Cookies (Ctrl+Shift+Delete) and restart the browser.
  • Pop-up Blockers: The RTC is usually generated in a new window. If nothing happens when you click "View" or "Download," check your browser’s address bar for a blocked pop-up icon and select "Always allow."
  • OTP Not Received: During registration or login, if you don't receive an OTP, wait 2 minutes before clicking "Resend." Ensure your mobile number is not on a DND (Do Not Disturb) list.

Payment & Download Issues

These are the most common frustrations, especially when money is debited but the document doesn't appear.

Issue Solution
Money Deducted, No PDF Go to the "Missed i-RTC" section on the portal. Enter your order number or transaction details to re-download the document without paying again.
Payment Status "Pending" This usually clears within 24 hours. Avoid making a second payment immediately. Check the "Check Payment Status" tool on the site.
PDF Not Opening Ensure you have a PDF reader (like Adobe Acrobat) installed. If the file is 0KB, the download was interrupted; try again from the "Missed i-RTC" section.

Data & Record Issues

Sometimes the website works fine, but the information displayed is incorrect or missing.

  • Survey Number Not Found: Ensure you are entering the Surnoc and Hissa numbers correctly.
    • Try searching by Owner Name instead of Survey Number to see if the record is indexed differently.
  • Name or Measurement Mismatch: If the owner's name is spelled wrong or the land area is incorrect, this cannot be fixed online. You must visit the Tahsildar office (Taluk level) with your original Sale Deed to apply for a Rectification Mutation.
  • Mutation Status "Pending": If you recently bought land, it takes roughly 45–60 days for the mutation process to reflect on the RTC. You can track this under the "Mutation Status" tab using your transaction ID.

Land Record Management in Karnataka

While the previous sections covered the operational mechanics of the Bhoomi portal—how to click, view, and download—effective land management in Karnataka requires a deeper understanding of the administrative ecosystem. The digitization of records is a monumental step, yet the interface between digital data and ground reality is where most citizens face challenges. The following guide addresses advanced scenarios, strategic troubleshooting, and the "unwritten" protocols of the Revenue Department to help you safeguard your assets effectively.

1. Understanding the Mutation Lifecycle

When you purchase land or inherit property, the change of name (Mutation) is not instantaneous, even if the system is digital. Understanding the human hierarchy behind the "Pending" status can help you intervene effectively when delays occur.

The Journey of a File

Once a transaction is registered at the Sub-Registrar's office (Kaveri portal), the data is pushed to Bhoomi. However, the approval process moves through specific tiers of the Revenue Department:

  • The Village Accountant (VA): The first point of contact. The VA verifies the transaction against existing records. If your application is stuck, it is often pending the VA's initial "Checklist" approval.
  • The Revenue Inspector (RI): The critical field officer. The RI is mandated to visit the property, verify possession, and serve notices to interested parties. Strategic Insight: If your status says "Pending for RI Inspection" for more than 30 days, a physical visit to the Nadakacheri to schedule the inspection is often necessary.
  • The Shirastedar: A supervisory check to ensure no legal disputes (court stays) are active on the survey number.
  • The Tahsildar: The final approving authority who digitally signs the mutation order.

Common Failure Point: The "J-Slip" mismatch. Sometimes, the data sent from the Sub-Registrar (Kaveri) does not perfectly match the Bhoomi database (e.g., a spelling error in the seller's name). This causes the auto-mutation to fail, requiring a manual "Rectification Application" at the Bhoomi center.

2. RTC: Advanced Column Analysis

Most users look only at Column 9 (Owner Name) and Column 12 (Crops). However, the legal health of your land is hidden in the obscure columns of the Pahani (RTC). Ignoring these can lead to disastrous valuation issues during resale.

Column 3: Nature of Possession & Tenancy

This is the most litigious column. If you see names listed here other than the owner, or references to "Genide" (Tenancy), it implies protected tenant rights under the Land Reforms Act. Warning: Even if you buy land with a clear Title Deed, if Column 3 has a legacy tenant entry that was not formally cleared by the Land Tribunal, you may be unable to convert the land or sell it. Always ensure Column 3 is clear or explicitly states "Self" (Swantha).

Column 9: The "Pyki" Complication

In ancestral properties, you may see the term "Pyki" next to an extent of land. This indicates a share within a larger undivided survey number. The Risk: "Pyki" entries are often vague regarding specific boundaries. You might own 1 acre "Pyki" in a 10-acre survey number, but which 1 acre? Without a "Tatkal Podi" (sub-division sketch) to fix the boundaries, "Pyki" owners are vulnerable to boundary encroachments by neighbors.

Column 11: Liabilities and Court Stays

Banks mark their liens here (hypothecation). However, look for "Court Stay" or "Injunction" notes. Sometimes, a stay order from a decades-old civil suit remains on the record long after the case is resolved because no one applied to remove it. You must file a request with a certified copy of the court judgment to the Tahsildar to clean this column.

3. "Phut Kharab": Why Your Map and RTC Don't Match

A frequent source of anxiety for landowners is discovering that their physical land measurement does not match the RTC extent. This is often due to "Phut Kharab" (B-Kharab) land.

Class A Kharab: Land that is unfit for agriculture due to geographical reasons (rocky terrain, water logging). This land belongs to you and can be converted for non-agricultural use.

Class B Kharab: Land reserved for public use (footpaths, cart tracks, canals, burial grounds). This land belongs to the government, even if it falls inside your survey boundaries. Strategic Implication: If your RTC says you own 2 Acres, but 0.10 Guntas is classified as "B-Kharab," you effectively only own 1.30 Acres for development purposes. You cannot build a wall around B-Kharab land. When buying land, always subtract B-Kharab from the total extent to calculate the true usable value.

4. Navigating Land Conversion: The Shift from Bhoomi to E-Swathu

Bhoomi manages agricultural land. Once you decide to build a house or a factory, you must convert the land use (DC Conversion). This transition is complex and often misunderstood.

The Transition Phase:

  1. Application: You apply for conversion online via the Revenue Department.
  2. Reports: The file is routed to the Urban Development Authority (CDP plan verification), Pollution Control Board, and Fire Department.
  3. The "Order": Once the Deputy Commissioner (DC) issues the Conversion Order, the land is technically no longer "Agricultural."

The Crucial Step: Once converted, the land record must eventually move out of Bhoomi and into E-Swathu (for rural panchayats) or E-Aasthi/UPOR (for urban municipal corporations). The Gap: Many owners get the conversion order but fail to mutate the record in the local municipality. This leaves the land in a "limbo" state—it appears as agricultural in Bhoomi (causing tax issues) but is legally residential. You must actively apply to the Panchayat/Municipality to create a "Form 9/11" (Khata) to finalize this shift.

5. Succession and Inheritance: The "Vamshavruksha" Protocol

Inheritance mutation (Pavati) is distinct from sale mutation. It relies heavily on the "Vamshavruksha" (Family Tree) certificate. A common bottleneck occurs when family members are estranged or abroad.

The "No Objection" Requirement:

If a father passes away leaving land to three children, the RTC will typically list all three names jointly. If one sibling wants to sell their share, or if the family wants to divide the property (Partition/Vibhag), all members must be present to sign or provide a registered Power of Attorney (POA). Pro Tip: If a legal heir is missing or uncooperative, the Revenue Department cannot adjudicate. You must approach the Civil Court for a "Partition Suit." Revenue officers are strictly administrative; they cannot decide on disputed inheritance claims.

6. Fixing Legacy Errors: The Akarband and Tippan

Sometimes the RTC is right, but the map is wrong. Or the map is right, but the RTC is wrong. In Karnataka, the "Mother Document" for measurements is not the RTC, but the Akarband.

Akarband: The final register of area and assessment. It was established during the original survey (often decades ago). Tippan: The field book containing the original hand-drawn sketches and measurements of the survey number.

Strategy: If you face a boundary dispute where a neighbor claims you have encroached on their land, or if the digital map in Dishaank seems shifted, do not rely solely on the RTC. Apply for a certified copy of the Tippan and Akarband from the Survey Department. These documents hold higher evidentiary value in court regarding boundaries than the Bhoomi RTC.

7. Security: Protecting Your Land from Digital Fraud

Digitization has reduced manual tampering, but "Identity Theft" in land records is a new threat. Fraudsters may present fake IDs to initiate a mutation or change the mobile number linked to the account.

The "Watch Mode" Strategy

Land is a passive asset, often ignored for years. This negligence is dangerous. Adopt a "Digital Watch" routine:

  • Quarterly Checks: Log into Bhoomi every three months to view your RTC. Check the "Mutation Status" tab even if you haven't applied for anything. If you see a "Pending" application you didn't initiate, file an objection with the Tahsildar immediately.
  • SMS Alerts: Ensure your current mobile number is linked to your Aadhaar and the land record. The system generates SMS alerts for new mutation requests. If you change your phone number, update it in the Bhoomi database via the outcome of a small correction application.

8. The "Podi" Service: Moving from Joint to Individual Ownership

A vast majority of rural litigation stems from "Joint Ownership" in a single Survey Number. If you and your brother own 2 acres each in Survey No. 10 (Total 4 acres), the RTC shows both names. This is legally weak.

The Solution: Tatkal Podi

You must apply for "Podi" (Sub-division). Government surveyors will come, measure the specific 2 acres you possess, and assign a new subdivision number (e.g., Survey No. 10/1 and 10/2). Why do it? Banks are increasingly refusing loans on "Joint" RTCs. Furthermore, without Podi, you cannot sell a specific piece of the land clearly. The "Tatkal Podi" scheme is a fast-track method (paid service) to get this done, usually within weeks compared to the months taken by the regular process.

9. Dispute Resolution Hierarchy

If the Tahsildar passes an order you disagree with (e.g., rejecting your mutation), do not rush to the High Court. The Revenue Act provides a specific appellate hierarchy:

Authority Role Time Limit for Appeal
Assistant Commissioner (AC) First Appellate Authority. Handles disputes regarding mutation orders and title entries. Usually within 60 days of the Tahsildar's order.
Deputy Commissioner (DC) Second Appellate Authority. Reviews the AC's decisions and handles major conversion disputes. Within 60 days of the AC's order.
Karnataka Appellate Tribunal (KAT) The final revenue court before the High Court. Varies, usually 90 days.

Note: Skipping these steps and going directly to the Civil Court is often rejected on the grounds of "Exhaustion of Administrative Remedies." However, for questions of title (who is the true owner), the Civil Court is the only authority; Revenue Courts only decide whose name goes in the record based on summary evidence.

10. Missing Records: When the System Says "Data Not Found"

A terrifying scenario for an owner is entering their survey number and seeing "Data Not Found." This usually happens for two reasons:

  1. The "UDR" (Update Data Registry) Issue: During the massive digitization drive in the early 2000s, some records were missed or entered with typos. These are "Missing RTCs." You must file an application at the Bhoomi center with your old manual (handwritten) Pahani to re-digitize the record.
  2. Survey Number Changes: Sometimes, during a re-survey, survey numbers are re-mapped or re-numbered. Use the "Village Map" to visually locate your land and identify the new survey number assigned to that geographic location.

11. Practical Tips for NRI/Out-of-State Owners

Managing Karnataka land from outside the state is difficult. The "Power of Attorney" (POA) is your most vital tool, but it has limitations.

  • Adjudication: A POA executed abroad (e.g., in the USA) is not immediately valid in India. It must be "Adjudicated" (stamped) at the District Registrar's office in Karnataka to be legally acceptable for property transactions or court appearances.
  • Life Certificate: If a senior citizen is the owner and cannot visit the office, some revenue services allow a video-KYC or a Life Certificate attested by a Gazetted Officer to process requests, though this depends heavily on the discretion of the local Tahsildar.

Summary Checklist for Healthy Land Records

  • Does your RTC match your physical possession?
  • Is the "Owner Name" spelled exactly as it appears in your Aadhaar/PAN? (Crucial for future sales).
  • Is the "Khata" (Account) number unique to you, or shared with others?
  • Have you downloaded the "Mutations" history to ensure the chain of title is unbroken from the previous owner to you?
  • Is the "Soil Type" and "Land Use" accurate? (Incorrect classification can lead to penalties).

By treating your digital land record as a living document that requires regular "health checks," you can avoid the nightmarish delays that plague property transactions. The Bhoomi system is powerful, but it rewards the vigilant.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Q: Can I use the online RTC copy for legal purposes?
A: The free viewable version is for information only. For legal matters, bank loans, or registration, you must download the Digitally Signed i-RTC, which is valid without a manual signature.

Q: What if my land details are missing on Bhoomi?
A: If your data is not digitized or contains errors, you must visit the nearest Bhoomi Monitoring Cell or your Taluk office to file an application for data entry or correction.

Q: How can I view the Revenue Map of my village?
A: Visit the "Revenue Maps" section on the Bhoomi portal, select your district/taluk/village, and you can download the PDF map showing survey number boundaries.
