Bhoomi Karnataka — Official Land Records Portal

Launched in 2000, Bhoomi was India's first state-wide land records digitization project — transforming 20 million paper records into a searchable public database.

What Is Bhoomi and What Does an RTC Contain?

The Revenue Department of Karnataka manages Bhoomi, and its core document — the RTC (Record of Rights, Tenancy and Crops), called Pahani (ಧವನೃ) in Kannada — is far more than a simple ownership certificate. It is the land's complete legal identity document. Understanding what each column records is essential before buying, selling, or applying for a loan against any agricultural land in Karnataka.

The Critical Columns in an RTC — What Each One Means

Most buyers only check Column 9 (owner name). The hidden legal problems are in Column 3 and Column 11. Ignoring these is one of the most common and costly mistakes Karnataka property buyers make.
  • Column 3 — Nature of Possession & Tenancy: The most litigious column. If you see names other than the owner here, or the word "Genide" (tenancy), it implies a protected tenant with rights under the Karnataka Land Reforms Act. Even with a clear Title Deed, a legacy tenant entry never formally cleared by the Land Tribunal can prevent you from converting or reselling the land.
  • Column 9 — Owner Name & the "Pyki" Complication: In ancestral properties you may see "Pyki" next to an extent of land, indicating a share within a larger undivided survey number. Without a sub-division sketch (Tatkal Podi) fixing boundaries, "Pyki" owners are vulnerable to boundary encroachments.
  • Column 10 — Extent of Land: Total area in Acres and Guntas, broken down by soil class and irrigation source. This is the official legal area — not what you measure with tape on the ground.
  • Column 11 — Liabilities: Banks mark their mortgage liens (hypothecation) here. Also look for "Court Stay" or "Injunction" notes. A decades-old stay may still appear even after the case was resolved — you must file a request with a certified court judgment copy to the Tahsildar to remove it.
  • Column 12 — Crops Grown: Seasonal crop details used for crop insurance, Kisan Credit Cards, and agricultural loan eligibility. An incorrect crop entry can affect your insurance claim payout.

Understanding B-Kharab and Phut Kharab Land

A frequent source of anxiety is discovering that physical land measurement does not match the RTC extent. This is often because of Kharab land within the survey number:

  • Class A Kharab: Land unfit for agriculture (rocky terrain, waterlogging). This land belongs to you and can be converted for non-agricultural use with DC Conversion.
  • Class B Kharab: Land reserved for public use — footpaths, cart tracks, canals, burial grounds. This land belongs to the government even if it falls inside your survey boundaries. You cannot build on it, wall it off, or use it privately.
Practical rule: Always subtract B-Kharab from the total RTC extent to calculate the true usable area before negotiating price per square foot. An RTC showing 2 Acres with 0.15 acres of B-Kharab means only 1.85 acres are actually usable.

How to View Your Bhoomi RTC Pahani Online

A precise walkthrough of the Bhoomi portal — updated for the 2026 interface.

Viewing the Free Informational RTC

  1. Visit the Official Portal
    Go to landrecords.karnataka.gov.in. No login required for a basic view. Use Chrome or Firefox — avoid Internet Explorer.
  2. Click "View RTC and MR"
    Find this in the Services section on the homepage. This opens the land record search form.
  3. Select Location from Dropdowns
    Choose District → Taluk → Hobli → Village in order. Always select from the dropdown — never type manually. A single spelling difference causes "No Record Found."
  4. Enter Your Exact Survey Number
    Type the survey number exactly as on your deed, including Surnoc and Hissa sub-numbers (e.g., 45/2 not 45-2).
  5. Click "Fetch Details"
    Your RTC displays on screen. This is for information only. For formal submissions — bank, court, Sub-Registrar — you need the paid i-RTC.
Technical tips: If nothing opens when you click View or Download, look for a blocked pop-up icon in your browser's address bar and choose "Always allow" for this site. Clear your browser cache (Ctrl+Shift+Delete) if the page does not load correctly.

Downloading a Digitally Signed i-RTC (Legally Valid)

An i-RTC (Integrated RTC) carries an official digital signature and QR code, making it legally valid for bank loans, court proceedings, and property registration. Most institutions require the i-RTC — not the free download.

  1. Log Into i-RTC Wallet Services
    Visit the Bhoomi portal and click i-RTC Wallet Services. Register with your mobile number and Aadhaar-linked OTP, or log in if you already have an account.
  2. Add ₹15 to Your Wallet
    Top up via UPI, Net Banking, or Debit/Credit Card. Screenshot your Transaction ID immediately — this is your only recovery reference if the download fails.
  3. Search and Select Your Land
    Enter District, Taluk, Hobli, Village, and Survey Number. The system displays your land record for review before finalizing payment.
  4. Pay & Download the PDF
    Click Pay and Print. Your signed i-RTC PDF downloads immediately. Save it securely — the download link expires. If money was deducted but PDF did not appear, use the "Missed i-RTC" section to re-download without paying again.
Best time to download: The Bhoomi server undergoes maintenance between 10 PM and 12 AM. Use the portal between 8 AM and 1 PM for the fastest payment gateway response and fewest errors.

Land Mutation in Karnataka — Complete Guide to Hakku Badlavalike

Registration at the Sub-Registrar is not the final step. Mutation is what makes you the official owner in government revenue records.

Why Skipping Mutation Is a Serious Mistake

Thousands of buyers register their sale deed and believe the process is complete. Without Hakku Badlavalike, the Bhoomi portal continues showing the previous owner's name indefinitely. The practical consequences:

  • Banks refuse agricultural loans because your name does not appear in revenue records
  • Property tax receipts continue in the seller's name, weakening your ownership paper trail
  • Future resale becomes legally complicated — title searches reveal a gap in the ownership chain
  • Government land acquisition compensation may be paid to the person named in the RTC, not you
  • If the seller passes away before mutation, resolving it requires dealing with all legal heirs
Apply within 90 days of registering your sale deed. Every month you delay increases the risk of complications.

How the Mutation Process Actually Works

Mutation moves through a specific hierarchy. Understanding who does what helps you intervene effectively when delays occur:

1
Village Accountant (VA) — Data Verification
The VA compares your sale deed details against existing Bhoomi records. A "J-Slip mismatch" — a spelling difference between the Kaveri portal and Bhoomi database — causes auto-mutation to fail here, requiring a manual Rectification Application. This is one of the most common silent failure points.
2
Revenue Inspector (RI) — Field Verification
The RI physically visits the site to verify possession and serves 30-day notices to interested parties. If your status shows "Pending for RI Inspection" for more than 30 days, a personal visit to the Nadakacheri is almost always needed to schedule the inspection.
3
30-Day Public Notice Period
A public notice invites objections from any claimants. If no objection is filed within 30 days, the mutation moves forward. If an objection is filed, the Tahsildar holds a formal hearing.
4
Tahsildar — Mutation Order & Database Update
The Tahsildar digitally signs the Mutation Order (MO), officially transferring the record to your name. The Bhoomi database updates and your new RTC is generated. Total time: typically 3 to 6 months.

Documents Required for Mutation

  • 📄 Certified copy of the registered Sale Deed (from Sub-Registrar)
  • 📋 Latest RTC (Pahani) showing the previous owner's name
  • ✉ Property tax receipt in the previous owner's name
  • 👤 Aadhaar card of the new owner (buyer)
  • 🗺 Land sketch/map (if required by the Taluk Office)
  • 📝 Affidavit (at the revenue authority's discretion)

Protecting Your Land from Digital Fraud

  • Quarterly check: Log into Bhoomi every 3 months and check the Mutation Status tab. An unexpected "Pending" mutation you did not initiate is a red flag — file an objection with the Tahsildar immediately.
  • Link your mobile: Ensure your current mobile number is linked to Aadhaar and the land record. Update it if your number changes.
  • Apply for Podi: If you share a survey number with others, apply for Podi (sub-division) to get your own individual survey number. Banks increasingly refuse loans on jointly listed RTCs.

Why Bhoomi Shows "No Record Found" — 8 Real Causes & Fixes

This message rarely means your land record is permanently missing. Here is exactly what it means and how to resolve each scenario.

Jump to a Specific Cause

  1. Incorrect or partial survey number format
  2. Hobli name mismatch in the database
  3. Data migration error from digitization
  4. Recent bifurcation not yet updated
  5. Land under court freeze or encumbrance
  6. Old survey number after a re-survey
  7. Village name recorded differently
  8. Server outage or technical glitch
Cause 01
Incorrect or Partial Survey Number Format

Karnataka survey numbers often include sub-divisions as fractions or letters — for example 45/2 or 67A. Entering 45-2 instead of 45/2, typing 67a in lowercase, or including/excluding leading zeros are all enough to produce "No Record Found."

Fix: Check your original sale deed, Pahani copy, or property tax document for the exact format. Try multiple variations. Also try searching by Owner Name instead of survey number — records are sometimes indexed differently.
Cause 02
Hobli Name Does Not Match the Database

The Bhoomi database stores hobli names in specific transliterated forms that may differ from older documents. "Yelahanaka" vs "Yelahanka" — a single character difference breaks the search entirely.

Fix: Always select your hobli from the portal's dropdown — never type it manually. If you cannot find it, visit the Taluk Office and ask which hobli your specific village is registered under in the revenue records.
Cause 03 — Most Common
Data Migration Error During Digitization

When Karnataka digitized its paper records in the early 2000s, thousands of entries were incorrectly transcribed — a survey number entered with a typo, a village name misspelled, or an entire sub-division accidentally omitted. Your land exists physically and legally, but the digital record is corrupted or missing.

Fix: File a formal complaint at your local Taluk Office using Form 9 (Correction of RTC) along with your original handwritten Pahani. The Revenue Inspector physically verifies the land and corrects the data. This takes 30–90 days but is the only permanent solution.
Cause 04
Recent Land Bifurcation Not Yet Updated

When a survey number is split between family members or sold in parts, the old number is closed and new sub-numbers are created. If the bifurcation was approved but not yet entered into Bhoomi, neither the old nor the new numbers appear.

Fix: Check with your Taluk Office whether the Mutation and Podi (bifurcation) order have been entered into Bhoomi. Request the Mutation Register copy to track where the record currently stands.
Cause 05
Land Under Court-Ordered Freeze or Encumbrance

Records can be temporarily locked in Bhoomi if there is an active court stay, a government acquisition notice, or an ongoing boundary dispute filed with the revenue office. During this freeze, records do not appear in public searches.

Fix: Consult a property advocate and confirm with the Taluk Office whether your land is under any legal hold. Certified copies of the RTC can still be obtained directly from the Tahsildar's office even when the portal does not display the record.
Cause 06
Old Survey Number After a Re-Survey Operation

Several areas in Karnataka have undergone complete re-survey operations assigning entirely new survey numbers. Searching with the old number returns nothing because it no longer exists in the current database.

Fix: Visit your Taluk Office and request the Re-Survey Concordance Table — a document mapping old survey numbers to new ones. This is not available online and must be obtained in person.
Cause 07
Village Name Recorded Differently in the System

With thousands of villages, many names were transliterated inconsistently during digitization. "Doddaballapura" vs "Doddaballapur" — one letter is enough to return no results.

Fix: Search without your village name first — select just District, Taluk, and Hobli, then browse the village dropdown to find the exact spelling used in the Bhoomi database. This eliminates transliteration errors entirely.
Cause 08
Server Outage or Technical Glitch

Bhoomi serves millions of users simultaneously. During peak hours, scheduled maintenance (typically 10 PM–12 AM), or after software updates, the portal may return "No Record Found" for records that exist and would display correctly at other times.

Fix: Use the portal during morning hours (8 AM–11 AM) or on weekends. Clear your browser cache. If the issue persists for multiple days, contact the Bhoomi helpdesk at 080-22113255 or visit the SSLR Building, K.R. Circle, Bangalore.

Advanced Land Record Topics in Karnataka

Critical knowledge for serious property owners, buyers, and NRI landowners.

RTC vs Mutation Register — The Real Difference

The RTC (Pahani) is a snapshot of the land's current status — who owns it right now, what crops are grown, the total area, and any active bank liabilities.

The Mutation Register (MR) Extract is the historical log of how ownership changed. Every time property was sold, inherited, or divided, a Mutation was recorded. Banks and lawyers demand MR extracts for the last 15–30 years to ensure the current RTC owner acquired the land through a legally valid chain.

Practical rule: The RTC tells you who owns the land today. The MR Extract tells you how they came to own it. You need both for proper due diligence.

Karnataka Land Measurement System Explained

When reading a Bhoomi RTC, measurements appear in the traditional Karnataka system built around the Gunta:

  • 1 Gunta = 1,089 square feet
  • 40 Guntas = 1 Acre (= 43,560 sq ft)
  • 2.47 Acres = 1 Hectare
  • 100 Cents = 1 Acre (some regions)

In older handwritten pre-Bhoomi documents, you may see fractions in "Annas" — 16 Annas make one complete share. So "8 Anna share" means exactly 50% of that survey number's area. This system appears in ancestral property documents and partition deeds.

Use the Land Area Converter tool above to convert between any of these units instantly.

After DC Conversion — The Step Most Owners Miss

Getting the Deputy Commissioner's (DC) Conversion Order moves your land from "agricultural" to "residential/commercial" legally. But many owners stop there — and it creates a serious problem.

Your land record in Bhoomi still shows it as agricultural. You must actively apply to the local Panchayat or Municipality to create a Form 9/11 (Khata), transferring the record from Bhoomi into:

  • E-Swathu — for properties in rural Gram Panchayats
  • E-Aasthi / UPOR — for urban municipal corporations

Without this step, you pay agricultural land tax on legally residential land, and banks will not give home loans because government records still classify it as agricultural.
